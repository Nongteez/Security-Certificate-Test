  - paragraph:
    - generic: The lecture recommends strong, unique passwords, no reuse across applications, no sharing, avoiding predictable personal details, appropriate password-manager use, MFA and reporting suspected compromise. Reuse lets a password stolen from one service be tried elsewhere; a unique password limits that particular route but does not stop phishing or malware by itself. Follow the approved policy and protect recovery methods too. Supplementary current NIST guidance rejects arbitrary periodic password changes and composition rules, and calls for screening unacceptable/common/compromised choices. Evidence of compromise requires action. This does not create one universal length or rotation rule for every system.
