  - paragraph:
    - generic: Business Continuity (BC) is the organizational capability to sustain critical business operations during and after a disruption. It can include a safe reduced service level or an approved temporary process, rather than an assurance that nothing ever stops. Redundancy provides alternate components, links, locations, people or suppliers for a specified failure. It reduces dependence on a single point only when the alternate is usable, sufficiently independent and capable of the required workload. Shared power, a shared provider or the same regional flood can disable both resources. Backups preserve recoverable data and configurations; redundant copies can replicate corruption, while a stored backup alone cannot staff an operating service. A documented BCP coordinates people, procedures, communications and recovery. Exercise the arrangements and assess the remaining dependencies.
  - list:
    - generic: BC coordinates people, procedures, facilities, suppliers and technology around the critical service.
    - generic: An alternate reduces a specified dependency; test independence, access and required capacity.
    - generic: A backup preserves recoverable data; it does not by itself deliver a working business service.
  - paragraph:
    - generic: Prepare an alternate, test the dependencies and keep recoverable data.
