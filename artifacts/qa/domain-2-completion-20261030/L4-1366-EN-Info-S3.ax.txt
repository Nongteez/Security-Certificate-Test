  - paragraph:
    - generic: "Recovery Point Objective (RPO) sets the maximum acceptable period of data loss, expressed in time. From an outage timestamp, subtract RPO to find the earliest acceptable recoverable data point. Recovery Time Objective (RTO) sets the target maximum time for restoring the affected service: add RTO to the outage timestamp to find the target deadline. Actual data loss and observed recovery time are measured results to compare with these objectives. Scheduled backup frequency alone does not prove RPO: failed jobs, replication lag, corrupt data or unavailable keys can make the last usable point older than expected. Test the recovery chain against both objectives."
  - list:
    - generic: "RPO (Recovery Point Objective — Backward Data Window): Defines the maximum acceptable age of lost data between the last good backup/replication point and the disruption."
    - generic: "RTO (Recovery Time Objective — Forward Downtime Window): Defines the maximum acceptable duration the IT system or application can remain unavailable after the disruption."
    - generic: "Why Backup Frequency Alone Does Not Guarantee RPO: If backups are scheduled every 30 minutes but replication lags, media is corrupted, or encryption keys are unavailable, actual data loss may exceed the target RPO."
