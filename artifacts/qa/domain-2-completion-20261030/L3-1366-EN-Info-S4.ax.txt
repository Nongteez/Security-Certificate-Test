    - generic: Explain how Business Impact Analysis (BIA) identifies critical business functions and recovery priorities, and distinguish the whole-business scope of BCP from the IT restoration scope of a Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP).

  - paragraph:
    - generic: Alternate work locations, backup personnel and alternate suppliers make continuity practical when the primary resources are unavailable. They still need safe access, approved authority, working communications and sufficient capacity. A second office in the same flood zone may share the original exposure. Two internet contracts can depend on the same physical route or upstream carrier. A named deputy may not have current skills, access or availability. Verify these dependencies, train replacement staff and test the alternate under a realistic workload. Essential technology and approved temporary manual processes can support a limited service while recovery continues. Protect sensitive information and reconcile temporary records afterward; moving to paper or a secondary site does not suspend security requirements.
  - list:
    - generic: Shared flood zones, power or upstream providers can defeat apparently separate resources.
    - generic: Replacement personnel need training, current access and appropriate delegated authority.
    - generic: Exercise temporary manual processes, protect their records and reconcile them when systems return.
  - paragraph:
    - generic: Separate resources are useful when their dependencies can survive the incident.
