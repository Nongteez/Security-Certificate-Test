    - paragraph:
      - generic: "A BCP documents how an organization will continue critical operations during a disruption. BC is the ability to continue important business work; a Business Continuity Plan (BCP) documents how people are to do it. Course slide S14 gives eight useful plan elements, all retained in the table: critical business processes, roles and responsibilities, communication procedures, alternate work locations, backup personnel, alternate suppliers, essential technology and systems, and recovery priorities. They are practical planning considerations, not a universally mandated eight-part template. An instruction needs enough detail to be usable: who may activate it, what service level is needed, where the team works, which tools and contacts remain available, and when the situation must be escalated. Connect the arrangements to the service and exercise them together. A plan can be updated as dependencies, staff and business requirements change."
    - list:
      - generic: BC describes the capability; the BCP describes the arrangements and procedures.
      - generic: Assign activation authority and responsibilities, then establish workable communication routes.
      - generic: Identify critical work, dependencies, alternatives and recovery priorities; exercise their coordination.
    - paragraph:
      - generic: A useful plan tells people what to do and how to coordinate.
