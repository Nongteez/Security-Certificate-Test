EN: Apply the AAA distinctions from Lesson 2 to the ticket AI introduced in Lesson 1. Register a service identity and verify its credential (Authentication). Check permission for the specific resource and action (Authorization): this example allows a priority update on T-104, not a billing change. Protect the event record (Accounting), including the service identity, action, target, time and result; record denied attempts as appropriate too. Keep relevant configuration/model versions and any required approval evidence so a reviewer can reconstruct the context. The owner and approving people remain responsible for the authorized deployment and oversight; a service identity does not identify which human intended each action. If a protected action record is digitally signed, verification can support its integrity and signing-key origin, and can support Non-repudiation with trustworthy identity-to-key binding, key custody, certificate trust when used and surrounding evidence. Shared or compromised credentials/keys weaken attribution. A log or valid service signature alone does not prove human intent, actual approval or legal liability. Human-assisted recommendations also need a record of the eventual authorized action; merely generating a suggestion is different from executing it. Logs themselves need access and retention controls; do not record unnecessary sensitive ticket content.
