# Full-course bilingual terminology review

**IN PROGRESS — 107/316 slides substantively reviewed; 209 pending. These are checkpoint reports, not final course approval.**

Reviewed vocabulary includes CIA (Confidentiality / การรักษาความลับ; Integrity / ความถูกต้องครบถ้วน; Availability / ความพร้อมใช้งาน), identification as a claim, authentication as checking evidence/control, authorization as permission and accounting as recording. Auditing reviews records; accountability is responsibility, not a synonym for a login event. MFA requires distinct factor types; biometric use is not standalone under the cited NIST digital-authentication model. Digital signatures support integrity and signing-key origin, with identity binding and custody needed for stronger attribution. Signing is not generic private-key encryption. Technical English is retained where helpful for exam preparation.

All reviewed paired fields, including claims, qualifiers and revealed explanations, are represented in per-slide review records. Meaning differences and editorial proposals are below; exact current/proposed EN/TH pairs and source evidence appear in the academic report and findings.json.

- FC-001 (LOW), lesson-d1-02-aaa-mfa/slide-05-factor-families, blocks.2.text: Thai uses an awkward double negative for forgeability. Meaning can be recovered but learners must parse two negatives.
- FC-002 (MEDIUM), lesson-d1-01-cia-triad/slide-02-cia-overview, visual.takeaway: Encryption can still protect confidentiality during an outage. Saying it cannot help or is useless conflicts with the later correctly qualified authenticated-encryption explanation.
- FC-003 (MEDIUM), lesson-d1-01-cia-triad/slide-02-cia-overview, visual.pillars.1.summary: Thai adds hashes as a means of preventing modification, whereas a plain hash helps detect change against a trusted baseline. English states the goal without that causal claim. Reading and expanded Info expose this sentence.
- FC-004 (MEDIUM), lesson-d1-01-cia-triad/slide-05-availability, blocks.0.text: Zero business value is an unsupported absolute. Temporarily unavailable data can retain future recovery, evidentiary or regulatory value. Teach the operational harm without asserting total loss of value.
- FC-005 (MEDIUM), lesson-d1-01-cia-triad/slide-08-takeaways, blocks.1.rows.2.3: Thai omits alone and reads as a blanket assertion that backup does not help confidentiality. English and Presentation correctly distinguish backup alone from separately protecting backup secrecy.
- FC-006 (LOW), lesson-d1-01-cia-triad/slide-08-takeaways, blocks.0.text: Common exam traps implies exam-frequency evidence that is not supplied. The official outline establishes scope, not frequencies or actual question patterns.
- FC-007 (MEDIUM), lesson-d1-01-cia-triad/slide-06-compound-impact, visual.lead: English says a complex intrusion can cascade across all three goals; Thai says it often does. Neither source establishes that frequency. The teaching need is possibility of multiple harms.
- FC-008 (LOW), lesson-d1-01-cia-triad/slide-09-ai-training-integrity, blocks.2.items.0: Thai duplicates the identical English term Data integrity in parentheses, adding noise instead of a helpful translation. The same pattern occurs in items 1 and 2.
- FC-009 (LOW), lesson-d1-03-non-repudiation/slide-06-evaluate-the-claim, blocks.2.text: Learner explanation refers to an original six-slide sequence although the published lesson now has seven slides. Remove stale authoring history and retain the evidence distinction.
- FC-010 (LOW), lesson-d1-03-non-repudiation/slide-03-sign-and-verify, blocks.2.text: Thai uses แบบเข้ารหัส for cryptographic digital signature. This can suggest encryption despite the correct preceding distinction; use an explicit cryptography term. This is a clarity issue, not a wrong key-role explanation.
- FC-011 (LOW), lesson-d1-09-frameworks/slide-06-standard-requirement, blocks.0.text: Thai translates adopted/adoption as รับใช้ (serve), an unnatural term for formally applying a requirement. The surrounding mandatory-condition reasoning is correct, but recurring wording weakens clarity of how a requirement becomes binding. Use นำมาบังคับใช้ consistently.
- FC-012 (LOW), lesson-d2-01-grc-foundations/slide-02-purpose-of-grc, blocks.1.rows.0.0: All seven purpose labels in the Thai Reading/Info table repeat English sentences without Thai glosses. Thai explanations mitigate impact and Presentation labels are translated, but this creates avoidable language inconsistency for Thai learners.
- FC-013 (MEDIUM), lesson-d2-01-grc-foundations/slide-03-importance-of-grc, blocks.3.items.0: Claims substantial prevention and greatly reduced penalty severity without evidence or jurisdiction-specific grounds. Learners may infer that documented GRC itself secures leniency. The source says helps avoid/reduce penalties, not a measured magnitude or assured enforcement outcome.
- FC-014 (MEDIUM), lesson-d2-01-grc-foundations/slide-03-importance-of-grc, blocks.2.text: Source S06 says organizations without effective GRC may have four failure modes. Published English makes them predictable breakdowns; Thai adds immediately. This changes possible consequences into inevitable immediate outcomes and weakens causal reasoning.
- FC-015 (MEDIUM), lesson-d2-01-grc-foundations/slide-06-applied-grc-scenario, blocks.3.items.1: Option B now mandates endpoint tools before governance policies. Reading/Info rationale instead criticizes a different option about treating ISO as law and stopping business. The feedback does not explain the actual distractor, even though the Presentation row-based feedback and correct C key are coherent.
- FC-016 (LOW), lesson-d2-01-grc-foundations/slide-05-grc-distinctions, blocks.2.text: English claims recurrence throughout ISC2 governance questions and Thai says frequently examined.
