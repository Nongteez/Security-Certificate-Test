# Domain 5 question-bank audit — Exam fidelity and quality rubric (F)

Date: 2026-11-03. Applies to **future independently authored practice questions**. Nothing here claims access to confidential ISC2 items, statistical equivalence, official endorsement, or an internal ISC2 cognitive taxonomy. [Blueprint](domain-5-audit-assessment-blueprint.md), [terminology](domain-5-audit-terminology-matrix.md), [findings](domain-5-audit-academic-findings.md).

## What fidelity can mean defensibly

The public outline establishes scope and entry-level expectations. It does not publish an exhaustive item-writing style manual, subtopic frequencies, answer-position distributions, or cognitive proportions. Four-option single-best-answer questions are the user's chosen practice format and match existing runtime conventions; official examinations also use other item types. Similarity to professional certification practice should be judged by precision, relevant reasoning, plausible alternatives and traceable learning value, not imitation of secret wording or unnecessary scenario length.

## Non-negotiable gates

Reject or return for repair if **any** condition holds. A numerical score never compensates for these failures.

1. Incorrect technical premise, unsupported correct answer or a rationale relying on an unresolved source claim.
2. More than one defensible answer under the stated facts; no defensible answer; missing essential timing, authority, policy or operational condition.
3. An option fails the requested answer type, is absurd/unrelated, duplicates another option, or cannot represent a credible misconception.
4. Objective association cannot be justified against 5.1–5.5, or excessive depth tests an unapproved enrichment target.
5. Explanation contradicts the key, omits a distractor's actual failure, or introduces a decisive fact absent from the stem.
6. Thai explanation changes certainty, actor, authority, scope, condition, negation, priority or correct-option mapping.
7. Substantial semantic duplication without a distinct assessed decision, or another item teaches/exposes the answer in the same assessment.
8. Wording, grammar, formatting, option length or response positions expose the key.
9. Confidential/dumped-exam provenance, falsely claimed official origin, or copied embedded source questions without independent authorship and quality review.

## Analytic rubric

Score each dimension 0–3: **0** invalid/absent; **1** material repair; **2** acceptable with recorded rationale; **3** clear and well-supported. Proposed acceptance requires no rejection gate, every dimension at least 2, and no unresolved material issue. Total 16–24 alone is insufficient. Scores organize review; they are not empirical validity coefficients.

| Dimension | Evidence required for score 2 or 3 | Repair signal |
|---|---|---|
| Technical support | Claim-level primary reference; original source location; version/conditions; defensible key | “Instructor said so” without checking an identified simplification |
| Objective/cognitive fit | One primary objective, named concept and decision, matching A–E category | Scenario looks complex but only expands a definition, or belongs mainly to D2/D3/D4 |
| Stem precision | Clear task, necessary actor/context/constraints only; enough facts to solve without seeing options | Unstated authorization, vague timeframe, ornamental BEST/FIRST, pronoun ambiguity |
| Option quality | Four unique parallel options of comparable specificity; three realistic misconceptions | Joke answers, inconsistent grammar, duplicate meanings, conspicuously detailed key |
| Single-best-answer proof | Independent evaluation of each option against the same criterion; counterexample search | “Most comprehensive” chosen without showing why other options fail the stated goal |
| Explanation value | Governing principle, why key meets conditions, why each other option fails, useful takeaway | Merely repeats the correct option, or uses facts not given in the question |
| Bilingual/English quality | Natural professional English; clear equivalent Thai reasoning retaining essential English terms | Literal translation obscures meaning; Thai removes uncertainty or changes priority |
| Diversity/leakage | Comparison with existing bank and candidate batch; answer-length/position/wording and cross-item review | Scenario substitution with same decision; obvious keyword matching; patterned keys |

## Reviewer responsibilities and actual independence

These are separate responsibilities, **not claims of separate human reviewers**. This audit used one assistant in one session and did not draft/review any future items. A future sequential review by the author must be recorded as self-review. A fresh reviewer is independent only if there was an actual separate review execution; human and native-Thai sign-off may only be recorded when obtained.

| Reviewer | Required work | Durable review record |
|---|---|---|
| A — Technical correctness | Check each factual assertion and its conditions against current primary references; reject unresolved F findings | Claim IDs, sections, version, corrections, confidence, PASS/REVISE/REJECT |
| B — Examination wording | Solve the English question unaided; evaluate actor, timing, task and qualifier; simplify unnecessary language | Inferred task, selected answer, missing facts, edited wording and rationale |
| C — Distractors | Evaluate all four options independently, including the key; identify misconception and tempting context for each wrong option | Per-option applicability, failure condition, alternate-answer counterexample |
| D — Bilingual accuracy | Compare reasoning and conditions across EN/TH, back-explain in independent words, check option letters after any movement | Parity checklist, disputed term, meaning differences, reviewer identity and independence |
| E — Coverage | Trace the item through claim → concept → opportunity → official parent; identify cross-domain overlap | Primary/secondary objectives, bullet description, A–E category, unique decision |
| F — Duplication/leakage | Compare all existing 702 items and the candidate batch, inspect answer cues and cross-item teaching | Ranked matches, human/assistant semantic verdict, rationale, unresolved cue repair |

Review records need real execution identity, date, input revision/hash, verdict, evidence and independence label. Do not fill six PASS cells with assumed outcomes. Re-solve after any material change; rerun answer/translation checks after moving options. Changes affecting a shared claim invalidate all dependent technical verdicts.

## Single-best-answer and distractor proof procedure

1. Hide options and state the required decision in one sentence. If the task cannot be identified, repair the stem.
2. For each option, record whether it addresses the task, whether it is true under the supplied facts, whether prerequisites hold, and whether it meets all constraints.
3. For the intended key, name the principle and the specific facts making it correct/best. For each distractor, name the relevant misconception and the exact failure/lesser suitability.
4. Try to make each distractor correct without changing stated facts. If a reasonable reading succeeds, add the missing condition or reject the item. Do not solve ambiguity by quietly adding assumptions in the explanation.
5. Confirm there is one surviving option and no shorter, equally valid unoffered interpretation that makes the stem misleading. Answer-length equality is not a substitute for technical comparison.

FIRST/NEXT items require the current stage, completed steps, decision authority, applicable procedure and relevant urgent constraints. Incident containment, escalation and evidence preservation can overlap. Avoid universal “always report first,” “always isolate first,” or “always collect all evidence first” claims. BEST/MOST appropriate must optimize a stated criterion; PRIMARY/MAIN must identify a distinct purpose; MOST likely must retain uncertainty. LEAST is exceptional and must justify its negative framing. The English/Thai qualifier matrix governs parity.

## Leakage and duplication checks

- Compare length, specificity, grammar and vocabulary across all options; flag a unique long/technical answer for inspection, not automatic acceptance/rejection.
- Check copying of uncommon stem phrases into only the key, article agreement, conspicuous parenthetical conditions, typographic emphasis and unsupported absolutes.
- Review key-position runs and distribution for predictability, then revalidate all mappings after deliberate editorial reordering. No fixed distribution is an official requirement; randomization cannot repair weak distractors. The current engine shuffles questions, so authored option positions still matter.
- Run normalized exact comparisons in both languages and token/similarity ranking for stems plus intended correct concept. Existing Jaccard/TF-IDF approaches are candidate finders, not proof of semantic uniqueness. Translate-only, paraphrased or different-industry scenarios may still be duplicates.
- Review pairs sharing concept + decision + governing principle, even when wording similarity is low. Keep multiple items only when constraints or reasoning materially differ; document the added value.
- Check the assembled form for cross-item answer disclosure. A practice explanation may teach a later concept intentionally, but an exam form must not give away another item's key.

## Bilingual explanation contract

Canonical stem and options are English. English explanation states the security principle and conditions. Thai explanation must teach the same decision process naturally while retaining key English terms. Each has one correct-option rationale and three option-specific distractor rationales. A short English takeaway supports review. Optional examples/tips must not introduce unverified rules or confidential-exam claims.

Use existing `explanation_detail.correct.{en,th}` and `.incorrect[].{letter,text_en,text_th}`; retain legacy explanation strings with matching reasoning. There must be exactly the three non-key letters; key index is integer 0–3. Thai prompt/option compatibility is a separate unresolved integration choice documented in the architecture report. Do not alter production schema or copy English into required Thai fields to disguise missing translations.

## Post-publication evidence, if later authorized

Pilot responses may reveal difficulty, discrimination, nonfunctioning distractors, response-time issues or unexpected alternative reasoning. No psychometric claims are available now. If collected later, record sample size and limitations; investigate negative discrimination and distractors never selected. Do not discard a technically sound foundational item merely to achieve a desired difficulty curve. Retain revisions and retired IDs so saved progress remains explainable.
